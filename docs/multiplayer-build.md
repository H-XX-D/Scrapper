# Recovery build — implementation and verification history

The runtime is integrated in `src/app.js`. `network.js` owns room discovery, PeerJS connections, input sanitization and snapshot sequencing. `crew.js` defines direction selection, PvP damage, co-op down/revive and respawn rules. `campaign-save.js` owns three solo slots and JSON import/export. `encounters.js` controls paced introductions and crew scaling. `world-complexity.js` adds stacked service paths; `mission-tasks.js`, `navigation.js`, `task-visuals.js` and `lighting.js` implement puzzles, routed compass, animated devices and pooled attack lights.

Transport correction: large JSON data-channel messages closed the guest connection when play began. Binary serialization now uses PeerJS chunking. Actual two- and four-browser rooms stayed connected with full enemy snapshots. Host/guest positions, ammo, pickups, tasks, radio choices, enemies, projectiles, effects and match outcomes share authoritative state. Guest disconnect removes the member without stopping the remaining crew. The host must remain connected; migration and in-mission joins are outside this build.

Art: 28 weapon-specific crew sheets cover four characters and seven guns. Each provides sixteen facing slots and two to four walking poses. Three sheets were regenerated after wrong source column counts were detected. Measured per-character crop rectangles replace assumptions about a uniform grid. `src/crew-layouts.js` records actual crops; source PNGs remain unmodified. Runtime inspection of 1,616 crew/effect frames found no empty frames or opaque crop-edge pixels. Front/back contact sheet: `artifacts/crew-weapons-contact.png`.

The opening contract has 93 placed enemies: co-op launches observed 186 with two players and 372 with four. Rival Recovery started at the single-player population with four connected salvagers, then its local spawners populated the active areas. FFA started with no actors or nests. Live shots damaged a rival; lethal damage awarded a frag and the rival respawned. A co-op downed EOS was revived by holding E on the host. Unit checks additionally cover revive interruption, mode-specific friendly fire, malicious/invalid input shapes and all four pure puzzle mechanics.

Solo browser checks recovered couplers, advanced the communication panel, saved a pending power choice, restored that choice, solved the pressure/relay objectives, extracted, and reloaded the completed mission without duplicating the campaign entry. Automated checks walk actual movement up stairs, ride both automatic lifts up/down, traverse the overhead recovery route, and verify mission-node reachability at its elevation. The complete chapter sequence has not been manually played for balance.

Latest visual steering: compact rooms and short branching connections, low ceilings on every deck, more infestation, no world signage except equipment-mounted terminal readouts. Terminal screens use cyan/pink/lime signals inside the original painted steel border. Padded and measured VFX frames replace square-clipped bursts. Real browser capture confirms visible head-on acid projectiles and nearby surface lighting.

Current release artifacts are `exports/Scrapper.html` and `exports/Scrapper-Playable-v3.html`. The v2 artifact is retained. Solo works offline from the self-contained file; multiplayer needs internet for room signaling and working WebRTC connectivity. The original `Scrapper-Playable-Checkpoint.html` is preserved as an earlier checkpoint, not the latest build.
