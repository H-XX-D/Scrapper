# Scrapper — current recovery design

Updated 2026-09-27. This document describes the implemented playtest direction. User corrections supersede older planning notes.

Keep the original title, steel pixel HUD, orange communication portrait, original enemy roster and existing artwork. First person is the only local camera. Character selection changes the local sleeve color and the multiplayer sprite. Remove tower defense, weapon leveling, dropships and upgrade economies. Scrap is score.

The level should feel close and infested. Standard passages have 3.6-unit headroom, rooms 3.8, and boss rooms 4.6. Vertical scale comes from stairs, automatic lifts and overlapping decks with their own low ceilings. Compact turns, junctions, service spurs and alternate routes replace long open connections. The map still has a substantial overall route and optional exploration, without inflating individual rooms. No room, corridor, door or lift signage is drawn. Functional readouts belong on terminals and use dayglo colors within the original industrial border.

The v3 mission loop starts with a chapter-specific recovery puzzle rather than the same two-coupler fetch. Twelve new mechanics cover circuits, cargo sorting, signal tuning, airlocks, cranes, memory, sensor-floor traversal, power sharing, temperature, specimen isolation, optical color mixing and timed shutdown. Four original mechanics remain as secondary systems. Twelve distinct connection graphs and room silhouettes retain low ceilings and overlapping decks. The compass tracks the player's actual route cell and deck, checks walkable shortcuts, and points to the next passage, stairs or lift landing. Recovery, research choices, guardian fights, secrets and extraction connect these systems to the existing campaign.

Rook is a salvage and recovery specialist whose routine job uncovers an infestation aboard Dock Nine. The shipping trail leads through research, cryogenic and manufacturing sites. Brood, Choir and Splice spread through different station systems. The union begins calling Rook as a specialist. The longer arc is in the recovery journal; mission discoveries, terminal actions and objective changes carry the story during play. Preserve/purge decisions change follow-on pressure and the ending. A saved game resumes its mission state and pending choices.

Combat should be dense and readable. The original seventeen ordinary enemy types and five bosses/minibosses have telegraphs, attack states and hit/death responses. More common weak creatures appear in groups; new types enter as the campaign advances. Pods, thorn growth and feeding mounds are active hatcheries, and corrupted fabrication vents release machines. Destroyed sources spew a final brood and retain their husk. Projectiles and impacts use pixel sprites; dynamic attack lights affect nearby architecture.

Seven fixed-capability weapons use separate ammo, selected by wheel or number keys after recovery from a supply case. Gun reloads start/end with the left hand supporting the fore-end. The muzzle converges on the crosshair. Each gun has a distinct layered sound, with separate world, weapon, UI and foley mix paths. Colored creature blood can overlap and mix. Q performs the original single wipe, with the mixed goo transferred onto the hand; there is no residual smear or second-wipe mechanic.

Online modes use up to four salvagers: Rook, Echo, Flint and EOS. Co-op shares mission progress, scales creatures by crew count and uses hold-E revival. FFA removes infestation and mission objectives, supports frags and respawns. Rival Recovery allows both player and creature combat while competing to extract the recovery. The host owns simulation and guests send validated input. Solo has three local save slots with portable JSON export/import.

This remains a playtest build: automated reachability, mechanics and browser connectivity checks do not establish that difficulty or every encounter is balanced. The next useful feedback is where navigation becomes unclear, pressure becomes exhausting, a sprite is unreadable, or a weapon feels weak. Preserve the visual identity while tuning those concrete problems.

Wall infestation is a destructible growing system: four pixel-art families, fixed wall anchors, maturity/pulse animation, nearby spreading, permanent destroyed husks and stronger flame damage. Its 96-patch cap keeps prolonged combat bounded. Area attacks use upright acid, shock and lightning sprites plus pooled lights, while acid impacts leave brief damaging pools. Flame shots use animated fire tongues and stronger traveling light.
